## Caption

Your promo spend is the first warning light on your casino catalogue.

It doesn't tell you what's wrong. It tells you where to look. The other four numbers tell you whether promotions are propping up a few titles or helping players discover the rest.

Five monthly checks, how to calculate each one, and the questions to ask your supplier before the next renewal.

Save it and run them on your own data.

## Slide

1. **Copertina a foto.** Kicker "Online casino · Catalogue". Five numbers that show whether your catalogue is working. What to check every month · Questions for your supplier
2. In my conversations with operators, I get far more questions about promo tools than about how to measure them. Which tournaments to run. How to switch on a jackpot. How many free spins to give. Much less often: what did they add to the result?
3. **Promotions are not a small line.** Pennsylvania iGaming, 2025. Before slot promo deductions: $3.46bn. Revenue reported by the regulator: $2.78bn. $688m gap. The gap, about a fifth, sits in online slots: largely promotional play the regulator lets operators deduct. It is not the full cost of promotions. It shows the order of magnitude. And the rules move. Since May 2025 Pennsylvania also allows deductions for promo play on live dealer tables, under conditions. If the regulator's perimeter can shift, fix your own.
4. **01 · Promo cost to GGR: the warning light.** Promo cost you booked this month ÷ GGR before promotional deductions. Count only what you fund: the share a supplier pays, such as a network tournament prize pool, stays out. Jackpots are never counted twice. Split acquisition from retention first: welcome offers push the ratio up whatever your catalogue does. Watch the direction, month on month, and read the two numbers apart. The ratio also rises when GGR falls. It doesn't give you the cause. It tells you where to look.
5. **02 · Concentration: share of GGR from your top 20 titles.** GGR of your top 20 titles ÷ total GGR. Add the same ranking by stakes or active players. Money and play don't always concentrate in the same place. A 2,000-game lobby where 20 titles carry most of the result is a 20-game catalogue with a long tail. Fine, as long as you know it. The risk is a share that climbs for months and nobody notices.
6. **03 · Supplier dependence: share of GGR from your top three.** GGR of your top three suppliers ÷ total GGR. Decide once whether a supplier is the studio or the aggregator, and keep that choice. Read slots and live apart. A delayed update, a withdrawn game, a renegotiation: the more three suppliers carry, the harder each one hits your month. A rising share means their commercial terms weigh more on you.
7. **04 · New games still played after 90 days.** Launches clearing your threshold in days 61 to 90 ÷ launches in the quarter. Wait until every title launched in the quarter is 90 days old. Set the threshold, on stakes or active players, before you look. Keep promo-driven play apart from organic play. Quarter on quarter, it tells you whether your choices on what to add, and how much lobby space to give it, are working.
8. **05 · Weighted RTP against observed RTP.** Sum of (theoretical RTP × stakes) ÷ total stakes, then compare with wins ÷ stakes. Use the RTP configuration live in the period. The result is the RTP of what was actually played, which can differ from your lobby average. The first number shows how the game mix weighs on margin. The gap with observed RTP shows volatility and volume. Read it on a rolling three months: a single jackpot can move one month on its own.
9. **Read them together.** Hypotheses to check, not verdicts. Acquisition, lobby position, seasonality and big wins move the same numbers. [Promo cost up · Concentration up · New games not holding] Check which titles absorb the budget. Promotions may be propping up your top titles instead of helping players find the rest. [Concentration high, stable · Promo cost flat or down · RTP in line] A narrow catalogue that works. No reason to add titles just to add them. [Promo cost flat · Top three suppliers up] Your exposure to their terms grows. Take it to the next renewal.
10. **Questions to ask your supplier.** 1. Can you give me GGR and stakes by title, month by month, in a format I can match with my own data? 2. Does your report separate the promo cost I carry from what you cover, including any contributions or co-funding? 3. Of the games you recommended that have passed 90 days, how many clear the threshold we agreed? 4. Can I export the configured RTP for each title, together with stakes? CTA: Save this and run the five numbers. The first three on last month, the other two on the last quarter.
