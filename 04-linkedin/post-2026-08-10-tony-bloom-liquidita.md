## Testo

Tony Bloom spends six months pricing options before he ever places a professional bet.

Nobody mentions that part. The poker nickname, the billion-pound fortune, Brighton's rise into the Premier League: the story gets told as a gambler's fairy tale. It isn't one.

Starlizard, the consultancy Bloom builds, runs on 160 people producing statistical models that price football matches more accurately than the bookmakers do. Weather, fatigue, squad rotation: all of it feeds the model before anyone takes a position.

He doesn't predict outcomes.
He prices risk better than the market does.

That's the distinction that gets lost when "professional gambler" becomes shorthand for luck. A trader who spends six months pricing options doesn't drop that discipline when the product shifts from equities to match outcomes. The mechanism stays the same: find where your price diverges from the market's, take the position, manage the exposure.

The same split shows up in how operators build a game portfolio, or how an aggregator decides what earns a slot in the catalogue. The ones who pick winners lose to the ones who manage a book of risk.

Where do you draw that line in your own decisions: instinct, or pricing?
