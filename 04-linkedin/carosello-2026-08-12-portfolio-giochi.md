## Slide

**1 — Hook**
Every casino catalogue promises thousands of titles. Players only ever see twenty.

**2**
Homepage space is fixed. The catalogue behind it keeps growing anyway.

**3**
Every title added starts costing the day it goes live: certification per market, hosting, QA. Whether it gets played or not.

**4**
A catalogue of five thousand and a catalogue of five hundred can show the player the exact same twenty titles on day one.

**5**
The operators that reach revenue fastest don't open with everything.

**6**
They open with what's already proven to work in a comparable market. The rest waits for demand, not the other way round.

**7 — Insight**

## Caption post (accompagna il carosello)

Every casino catalogue promises thousands of titles. Players only ever see twenty.

Homepage space doesn't grow with the catalogue. It stays fixed while the count behind it climbs.

Every title added after that starts costing before it ever gets played: certification per market, hosting, ongoing QA. The bill doesn't wait for the game to earn its place.

I've watched operators open with the opposite instinct and reach revenue faster: not the full catalogue on day one, but a curated set of titles already proven in a comparable market. The rest stays available. It just isn't first.

Volume was never the differentiator. The twenty titles you put in front of a player on day one are.

Swipe through, then count how many titles in your own catalogue have never been seen by a single player.
