## Testo

Brazil sold five-year betting licences at R$30 million each. They are now set to end next month.

On 25 September President Lula signed Provisional Measure 1.394, and Brazil's betting ban took effect the same day. It covers fixed-odds betting nationwide, sports and online casino, online and in physical outlets, including operators abroad serving players located in Brazil. Other legally authorised lotteries remain permitted.

The timetable is short. New deposits were prohibited from publication. Ads and sponsorship have to come down by 5 October, and sites and apps go offline from 6 October. Bets still unsettled at that point are void and refunded in full, winnings already determined are paid, and banks return balances to each player's own account. On 25 October the authorisations are extinguished.

Article 4 is the line I read twice. The measure ends them "for reasons of public interest", with no refund of the licence fee, in whole or in part, and no compensation. The SPA's 2025 review reported around R$2.5 billion in authorisation fees.

The numbers show what is being switched off. In its first regulated year the market generated about R$37 billion in GGR, with 25.2 million Brazilians betting through 79 authorised companies. The government's case is household debt and public health. ANJL, the operators' association, warns that more than 30 million players could move to illegal sites, in a country that has blocked 66,482 illegal domains since January 2025.

Six weeks ago I held Brazil up as the example of enforcement at scale. Same tools now, wider target. Banks and payment schemes, instant payments included, can no longer process betting transactions, except to wind down and refund players. App stores and operating systems that don't comply face fines of up to 10% of their group's Brazilian revenue.

The ban is already law, but its longer-term fate is open. Congress has until 23 November, extendable once by 60 days with the clock paused over the recess, to pass it into law, amend it or let it lapse. Amendments can only be tabled until 1 October. Operators are preparing a Supreme Court challenge.

Last week I wrote that a licence tells you where you can operate, not what you can offer. Brazil adds a harder lesson: a five-year gambling licence does not guarantee five years of market access.

The two graphics show how Brazil got here and the shutdown timetable.

If Brazil is anywhere in your 2027 plan, save this and check back as Congress and the courts move.
