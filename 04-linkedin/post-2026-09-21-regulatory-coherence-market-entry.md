## Testo

Most regulated markets share the same blind spot in a due diligence checklist.

Does the license exist. What's the tax rate. How long does certification take. Standard questions, answered in a spreadsheet before anyone signs anything.

What almost never gets asked: who owns making the rules cohere over time.

A market can have five different bodies, a gambling authority, a data protection authority, a consumer body, a tax office, a ministry, each holding one piece of a roadmap, with nobody assigned to assemble it into something an operator can plan against. On paper it looks identical to a market where one body owns the roadmap end to end. In practice the two behave nothing alike.

The first kind looks stable right up until a gap between two pieces becomes your problem: a requirement one body assumes another already covers, a deadline that shifts because no one had the authority to hold it fixed. The second kind lets you price that risk before you commit, because there's one place to go find out what changes and why.

It's the pattern I keep seeing when operators evaluate a new regulated market. The checklist gets ticked. The coherence question never gets asked.

The gap rarely shows up at entry. It shows up eighteen months in, in the account nobody wanted to own.

I break down what this means for choosing a game aggregator across more than one regulated market in this week's edition of The Betting Edge, in Italian. Link in the comments.
