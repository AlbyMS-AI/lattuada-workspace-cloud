## Testo

Getting the licence is when a regulated market starts changing under you.

Germany gives the clearest recent example. Since 1 July 2026 the regulator allows stakes above the ordinary €1 cap on virtual slots: up to €3 for players aged 21 or over, up to €5 after a 90-day qualification period with no signs of problem gambling and ongoing behaviour monitoring. The operator needs the regulator's approval, and the whole scheme is temporary, until 31 December 2027.

Read as a headline, German stake limits went up.

Read as a rule, it's a set of conditions: which players, after how long, under whose approval, until when. The headline changes nothing on its own. The conditions decide who can actually use it, and whether it's worth building anything around a window that closes in fifteen months.

That's the part of market entry that rarely makes it into the plan. Tracking every change after you're in, and for each one, the conditions that make it usable and the date it expires.

Three questions I put to any new rule before acting on it:

1. Is it the ordinary rule, or a derogation from it?
2. What conditions does it attach to the player, the operator or the product?
3. When does it expire, and what happens the day after?
