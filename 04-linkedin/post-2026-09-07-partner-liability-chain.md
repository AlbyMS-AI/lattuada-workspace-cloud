## Testo pubblicato

A partner signs the SLA. A sub-provider misses the deadline. Nobody agrees who pays.

Same story in five regulated markets: Italy, Belgium, Czechia, Germany, Ireland. Different regulators, same structural gap.

Layered deals are everywhere in iGaming: an operator signs with a partner, and the partner brings in third-party providers or sub-operators it doesn't control end to end. The contract still gets written like it's between two parties.

Three cracks show up every time.

First, the payment guarantee nobody can credibly give. A partner selecting third-party operators it doesn't collect revenue from directly cannot underwrite their payment risk. Ask for that guarantee anyway, and the number on paper stops matching what happens the day one link in the chain fails.

Second, the penalty clause aimed at the wrong party. Integration delays get penalized as if the signer controls the technical execution, when a subcontractor two steps removed is the one actually shipping the work.

Third, the contractual clock. Standard windows, two, three, five days, assume a negotiation between two parties. A chain with three or four links needs more time to coordinate than that clock allows, and the contract rarely adjusts for it.

None of this is anyone's fault along the chain. It's a design flaw in the layered model itself, not in any single agreement inside it.

Whoever writes the contract for the chain, not the pair, gets there first.
