## Testo

Every aggregator pitch opens with the size of the game catalogue.

In a multi-market expansion, it's the least useful number on the slide.

Take Germany. Game suppliers don't need a licence there. The filter sits on the product instead: operators need a separate approval for every virtual slot they offer, and stakes are capped at €1 a spin. A title built for other markets doesn't adapt with a setting. The studio rebuilds it, or it stays out.

Italy and Belgium put their filter somewhere else in the chain, one on the single game, one on the supplier itself. Same catalogue, three different answers to the question that matters on day one: how much of this can I actually switch on here?

An aggregator that performs well in one regulated market has proven it clears one filter. That tells you very little about the next two.

So before you compare catalogues, ask for three numbers, one per market, each with the date it refers to. If nobody can give them to you, that's an answer too.

I go through where each market puts its filter in this week's edition of The Betting Edge, in Italian. Link in the comments.
