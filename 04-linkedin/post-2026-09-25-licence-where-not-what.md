## Testo

A gambling licence tells you where you can operate. It doesn't tell you what you can offer.

Take Germany. Every virtual slot needs its own approval before an operator can offer it. A spin must last five seconds on average. The ordinary stake cap is €1, and since July the regulator allows up to €3 or €5, but only for players who meet specific conditions, with its approval, and only until the end of 2027. Deposits are capped at €1,000 a month across all operators, unless further checks allow more. And the virtual slot tax is charged on stakes, not on gross gaming revenue.

None of that is written on the licence. All of it shapes the product, the players you can serve and the margin you keep.

Italy and Belgium draw these lines in other places: how each game gets approved, which licence category unlocks which games, who in the supply chain needs its own authorisation.

When I look at a new regulated market, I read four layers together. What you can offer. To whom, and up to what limit. What tax leaves of the margin. Who you're allowed to work with.

I compare the four layers across Italy, Germany and Belgium in this week's edition of The Betting Edge, in Italian. Link in the comments.
